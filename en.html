Why it matters

Traffic signals still shine just as brightly at midnight as at noon

Brightness set for full daylight causes driver glare at night and light-pollution complaints from nearby homes. The need for dimming has long been recognized, but cheap sensors are inaccurate, precision light meters are expensive, and controller-based half-wave dimming requires changes to other safety equipment, so none of these approaches spread in the field.

7,204 per yearAverage artificial light pollution complaints in Korea (Ministry of Environment, 3rd Light Pollution Prevention Plan)
August 2025The Police Agency LED signal standard (R02) added a light sensor performance limit (f1′ within 8%). CdS sensor designs do not meet it.
846,141 facesTraffic signal faces installed nationwide (2026 Annual Traffic Safety Report). Main signals are required at every intersection.

How it works

One sensor sets every signal on an approach to the same brightness

1

Precision light measurement

A light sensor inside a cubic diffuser box measures brightness from two or more directions and derives one representative value for the intersection. Sun or a single nearby light source cannot skew it.

Light sensor box
2

Group control

The red, amber, arrow and green signals on the same approach are dimmed together from that representative value, so there is none of the uneven brightness that comes from fitting a sensor to each signal.

Day and night comparison
3

No new wiring

A Sensor SMPS inside the signal combines the sequentially switched lamp supplies through diode OR-ing to create continuous power. Existing controllers and cabling stay as they are.

System configuration

If the sensor unit fails, the signal simply lights at normal daytime brightness. Units are currently supplied with two-level day/night control under the Police Agency standard; if the standard adds finer levels, they can switch to multi-level control without changing the core technology.

Field trial: KICT SOC Demonstration Center, Yeoncheon

Operated in a real intersection environment for about 35 days, from July 25 to August 31, 2024. Measured light levels matched sunrise and sunset times from the Korea Astronomy and Space Science Institute, and the system ran reliably in clear, cloudy and rainy weather.

In subject glare ratings (5 = very glaring, 3 = appropriate), daytime brightness at night scored 4.1–4.5, while dimming to 42% of daytime brightness scored 3.2, rated appropriate.
